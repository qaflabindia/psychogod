# Judicial judgment, dignity and public access in India

## Pilot brief for judicial academies

A person who comes to court should be able to understand what the court decided, which facts mattered, and what happens next. For this programme, judicial dharma means the public duties of hearing, reason-giving, restraint and independent judgment. A participant may draw on a religious or secular account of these duties. Assessors score the conduct a litigant can observe. This is a proposed educational pilot. The materials have no regulatory, diagnostic or disciplinary standing.

## Legislative office and courtroom advocacy

On 25 September 2018 the Supreme Court dismissed a petition seeking to prohibit MPs, MLAs and members of legislative councils from practising law during their terms. It held that membership of a legislature did not make someone a full-time salaried employee under Bar Council Rule 49. Receiving statutory remuneration was insufficient to establish an employer–employee relationship. A court cannot currently refuse to hear an advocate solely because the advocate is an elected legislator on the strength of that rule. Separately, the Advocates Act regulates rights of practice and professional misconduct. [2018 judgment](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2018/4452/4452_2018_Judgement_25-Sep-2018.pdf) · [Advocates Act, especially sections 30, 35 and 49](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/1631/1/A1961_25.pdf)

**Policy for consultation.** Parliament could consider a prospective amendment suspending the right of sitting MPs and state legislators to appear as advocates for other people during their terms. Specify whether it extends to tribunals, unpaid appearances and instructions issued through another advocate. Include both houses where applicable, protect the member’s own access as a litigant and provide orderly substitution of counsel, file transfer and protection of client deadlines. Publish a procedure for verification, notice, response, appeal and resumption of practice. A litigant should have time and help to secure replacement representation. The proposal requires constitutional review, including equality, professional freedom and proportionality. Private advice, firm profit-sharing and indirect briefing need express treatment if the purpose extends beyond appearances.

The strongest argument for a term-long restriction is that a legislator can simultaneously scrutinise a department and represent a client against it, or seek procedural accommodation owing to legislative duties. The objection is that the same rule reaches unrelated matters and affects a client’s choice of counsel. Test the case for breadth with evidence of conflicts, frequency, public confidence and effects on representation in smaller bars. Compare a narrower regime of disclosures, matter-specific prohibitions, restrictions on publicly funded briefs and ordinary listing criteria. Neither a political affiliation nor a press report establishes professional misconduct.

## Docket pressure, political litigation and access

At 9 March 2026, the government reported 4,95,49,089 pending cases in subordinate courts and 64,01,789 in High Courts. Those figures measure a stock. They cannot establish which new filings, adjournments or listing decisions produced the delay. Parliamentary data for centrally supported MP/MLA criminal courts describe one subset of prosecutions, with additional state courts outside that group. A prosecution of an elected member, a constitutional challenge, a party’s election dispute, a publicity-driven PIL and an ordinary wage case argued by an MLA are different categories. [Rajya Sabha pendency answer](https://www.doj.gov.in/static/uploads/2026/04/b7b4da487543be9a9f1d1558d06d76d2.pdf) · [Lok Sabha answer on MP/MLA courts, pp. 264 onward](https://eparlib.sansad.in/bitstream/123456789/2992954/1/lsd_18_V_08-08-2025.pdf)

A political subject can require urgent scrutiny. In 2023 the Supreme Court directed High Courts to monitor criminal cases against legislators. In the Judge Loya litigation, the Court warned against using PIL to settle business or political scores. In *Bandhua Mukti Morcha* a letter about quarry workers brought allegations of bonded labour to judicial attention. The Court investigated and directed relief. Screening should examine the underlying injury, evidence, standing where relevant, urgency, earlier proceedings and availability of effective remedies. A blanket rule against petitions prompted by letters would hurt the very workers the access reforms aim to serve. [MP/MLA trial monitoring](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2016/29079/29079_2016_1_1501_48166_Judgement_09-Nov-2023.pdf) · [Judge Loya judgment, paragraphs 71–74](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2017/40744/40744_2017_Judgement_19-Apr-2018.pdf) · [Bonded labour decision](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/595099/)

**Audit design.** For six months sample comparable benches and publish aggregated median and 90th-percentile days to first effective hearing, exceptional listings granted and refused, hearing minutes, adjournments by reason, prior age and number of cases displaced from the same list. Classify the legal subject separately from counsel’s or a party’s public office. Compare similarly urgent cases over the same court, subject and period. Record missing information and suppress cells that identify vulnerable litigants. Ask court users whether they understood the result, had counsel when needed, incurred repeated travel or lost wages. An urgent listing after an arrest or before an election proves little by itself. A repeatable difference after matching warrants investigation. For practical access, direct eligible parties to the District Legal Services Authority or NALSA’s toll-free 15100. [NALSA eligibility and assistance](https://nalsa.gov.in/faqs/)

## The court-as-post-office failure

| Context | Judicial duty and limit | Exercise |
|---|---|---|
| Discharge and framing charges | In *Union of India v. Prafulla Kumar Samal* (1978), the sessions judge could sift the material to identify sufficient grounds for proceeding. The judge must independently assess the threshold. Grave suspicion may suffice. Testing credibility as at the end of trial exceeds the inquiry. [Judgment](https://pub-138b930c26524cb4bf6e9099898947c0.r2.dev/judgments/1978/Union_Of_India_vs_Prafulla_Kumar_Samal_Anr_on_6_November_1978_1.PDF) | Mark the material supporting each element, distinguish speculation from grave suspicion, and explain the result without prematurely deciding guilt. |
| Arrest and remand | *Arnesh Kumar v. State of Bihar* required the magistrate to examine the arrest grounds and record satisfaction when authorising detention under the provisions considered there. Police production of a checklist cannot supply the judicial decision. For current matters identify the law and transitional provisions governing the particular case. [Judgment](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/147520973/) · [BNSS, especially sections 35, 187 and 531](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/21544/1/the_bharatiya_nagarik_suraksha_sanhita%2C_2023.pdf) | Hear the detainee, identify the statutory basis and explain whether the material supports detention. |
| Writs directing an agency to consider a representation | *A.P.S.R.T.C. v. G. Srinivas Reddy* (2006) examined routine directions to consider representations. A vague direction may create another round of litigation or be read as endorsing the claim. Identify the legal entitlement, competent authority and question genuinely left for it. [Judgment](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/1504350/) | A worker has already received two acknowledgments. Decide whether the court can resolve the issue or should give a precise direction. Where appropriate name the decision-maker, deadline, point for determination and communication obligation. |
| Public-interest letters | In *Bandhua Mukti Morcha*, accepting a letter gave workers access, and judicial investigation and tailored relief followed. A court can accept an informal complaint and still exercise independent judgment about proof, jurisdiction and remedy. [Judgment](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/595099/) | Separate fact allegations from relief sought. Identify what requires verification and how the respondent can answer. |

For any exercise, the observer asks what the judge independently decided, what law authorised that step, what material supported it, whose answer the court heard and who is responsible for the next action. Brevity can serve a litigant. Copying a request without evaluating the statutory threshold cannot.

## Case conference and clinical boundaries

Use *ADM Jabalpur* (1976) and its repudiation in *Puttaswamy* (2017) to examine how a loss of access to habeas corpus affects an individual in detention. Use Hadiya’s marriage case to ask when a court’s protective jurisdiction displaces an adult’s own decision. Use *Election Commission v. Vijayabhaskar* for the distinction between oral remarks and a formal order, media freedom and the practical limits of expunging words outside the record. Use the 2024 Karnataka courtroom remarks proceeding to practise verification, a fair response and public repair. In discussing disagreement over faith or doctrine, score the participant’s capacity to give the opposing position a fair hearing. [1976 decision](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/165099707/) · [2017 decision](https://cdnbbsr.s3waas.gov.in/s3ec0490f1f4972d133619a60c30f3559e/documents/aor_notice_circular/43.pdf) · [Hadiya decision](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/18303067/) · [Election Commission decision](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2021/11474/11474_2021_35_1502_27915_Judgement_06-May-2021.pdf) · [Karnataka remarks decision](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2024/43583/43583_2024_1_301_56068_Judgement_25-Sep-2024.pdf)

For a contemporary discussion, the May 2026 reporting linked CJI Surya Kant’s insect comparison to a wider group during Sanjay Dubey’s hearing. The CJI subsequently explained he meant people with fraudulent qualifications. The evidence reviewed does not establish that he directly called the petitioner a cockroach. Ask whether the language was needed even accepting the clarification. There is no fixed personal payment to a petitioner for an insulting remark. Section 3 of the Judges (Protection) Act generally protects judicial acts and words, while preserving competent authorities’ powers under applicable law. Participants can practise correcting an unnecessary characterisation and restoring the person’s opportunity to answer. [Hearing report](https://indianexpress.com/article/legal-news/cji-surya-kant-youngsters-cockroaches-media-activists-rti-lawyer-10691246/) · [CJI clarification](https://indianexpress.com/article/legal-news/cji-reiterates-cockroach-remarks-only-for-fake-law-degree-holders-not-all-youth-10802482/) · [Judges (Protection) Act](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/1858/1/198559.pdf)

## Six-month implementation

| Month | Participants and work | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sponsor appoints faculty and independent assessors. Obtain voluntary pilot enrolment, accessibility preferences and consent. Orient participants in each working language and collect baseline performance in parallel scenarios. | De-identified baseline, accessibility check, declared rater conflicts and participant-selected development objective. |
| 2 | Trial and appellate judges lead case conferences on jurisdiction, threshold and limits of relief. | Short speaking orders and a reasoned response to the strongest contrary submission. |
| 3 | Practise remand, charge framing, repetitive representations and accessible routes for workers. Use role-play with a party appearing in person. | Written decisions and an observation of the opportunity to answer. |
| 4 | Psychiatrist and psychologist teach escalation, fatigue, interruption, repair and self-regulation. Judges rehearse withdrawing a harmful remark under time pressure. | A recorded simulation where authorised, participant reflection and confidential coaching. |
| 5 | Conduct peer observations or recorded simulations where live observation is inappropriate. Offer multilingual feedback and a repeat trial of a specific skill. | Two observations with behavioural examples, participant response and one actionable coaching goal. |
| 6 | Two independent assessors score fresh cases using the same anchors. Moderate disagreement and review possible bias. Offer written reasons, review and coached reassessment. | Participation or training-completion certificate issued by the academy. Report performance descriptively during the validation pilot. Repeat observation six months later to evaluate transfer. |

## Assessment protocol and governance

1. **Constructs.** Assess comprehension of opposing submissions, evidential reasoning, jurisdiction, proportionate use of authority, dignified language, independence from status or publicity, ability to correct an error and clarity of next steps. Anchor each rating in observed words or conduct. Never infer a syndrome, private motive or mental-health status from a score.
2. **Administration.** Standardise prompts, time and available materials. Rotate equivalent scenarios across training and final assessment. Ask assessors to rate independently before discussing. Capture the observed act, its effect on the opportunity to be heard and the written reason. Mark an unavailable observation as missing. Ensure interpreters and disability accommodations preserve access to the same underlying task.
3. **Validation.** Pilot with trial and appellate judges, legal-aid practitioners and court users across languages and regions. Conduct cognitive interviews and translation review. Check agreement between raters using an ordinal measure appropriate to the scale. Report estimates and uncertainty by competency. Examine task difficulty, repeated-performance consistency, and fairness across languages and accommodations where numbers allow. Compare scores with independent observations of ordinary hearings. Document weaknesses and revise before setting any consequential threshold. A successful workshop does not establish predictive validity. [Educational and psychological testing standards](https://www.testingstandards.net/uploads/7/6/6/4/76643089/9780935302356.pdf)
4. **Roles.** The academy is responsible for education and training records. A psychiatrist may design and teach wellbeing modules and, in a separate confidential relationship, offer clinical assessment where individually indicated. A psychometrician oversees validation. An independent panel with trial judges, court users, accessibility expertise and language representation hears assessment challenges. Judiciary-level governance and lawful authority must precede any compulsory national adoption. [National Judicial Academy](https://www.nja.gov.in/ITEC_Programmes-H.html)
5. **Privacy and response.** Use simulated proceedings wherever possible. Obtain appropriate authority and consent for recordings. Restrict individual records to authorised educational staff and the participant, set a retention and deletion schedule before enrolment and publish aggregated results. Agree in advance on procedures for a serious immediate safety risk or evidence of misconduct. Ordinary appeal, complaint and disciplinary channels retain their own standards and evidence. An educational certificate cannot displace constitutional procedures.
6. **Decision to expand.** Publish the number enrolled and completing, missing data, assessor agreement, variation across languages, accessible user feedback and follow-up outcomes. Include adverse effects such as excessive formality, longer hearings or discouraged complaints. An independent review should explain which findings justify expansion, what needs redesign and whether the pilot achieved better access for ordinary court users.
